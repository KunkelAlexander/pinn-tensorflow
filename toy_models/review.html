Nonuniform operators: what is actually ill-conditioned?

A learned operator inherits missing information from its inputs, but it does not necessarily inherit the condition number of a particular global Fourier reconstruction. Match the representation to the mesh before adding an optimizer.

Critical follow-up, 1 November 2026. Three new experiment groups and a focused primary-source review. The original report and results remain available. The conclusions below supersede its broad preference for a fixed reference Hamiltonian.

Sampling, coordinates and operator conditioning are different problems

Let S map a physical field to its sampled values. To predict an output F(f), the relevant question is whether there is a controlled bound

||F(f) - F(g)||_output <= C ||S(f-g)||_input

on the actual class of admissible fields. For a linear target L, the ratio is ||Lv||/||Sv||. A large condition number for a Fourier sampling matrix answers this question only for that Fourier space, those norms, and that target. Normal equations square the condition number; using QR or SVD avoids that additional numerical damage but does not recover unobserved information. A forward NUFFT is an evaluation algorithm, not an intrinsically unstable inverse. FINUFFT inverse tutorial.

The earlier diagnostic omitted two essential controls: a coarse background mesh under the halo points, and a basis with fine functions confined to the refined region. I added both. All rows use 128 samples. Fourier functions span modes −25 through 25 (51 real degrees of freedom). The alternative uses 50 elementwise Legendre functions: degree four on eight small central elements and two outer elements. Both bases are orthonormal in physical L2(dx/(2π)); sample weights are periodic Voronoi lengths. These weights define a norm rather than manufacturing an exact Fourier inverse.

The same halo_base20 grid gives approximately 10¹⁶ versus 4.26 depending on the approximation space. Conditions near 10¹⁶ are at double-precision resolution and should not be read as precise estimates. Keeping 52 well-spaced background points makes the 51-mode Fourier problem well-conditioned, at the price of resolving its bandwidth everywhere. The local basis itself becomes rank deficient if the samples fail to cover its small elements, as in the uniform row. Locality alone is no cure either.

Good conditioning is not approximation accuracy. On independent Gauss quadrature, the local space approximates a narrow carrier-40 packet with 6.6% relative error, while the Fourier space misses almost all of it. For a global carrier-20 wave, Fourier is exact and the local space has 89% error. This local space has intentionally coarse exterior resolution; its conditioning result does not authorize putting arbitrary high-frequency exterior waves into it. It is also a discontinuous representation diagnostic, not a PDE method with interface fluxes.

A concrete information limit. On halo_base20 a normalized Fourier wave v has weighted sample norm 3.99e-08. The two complex fields 1+i v and 1−i v have equal mass and identical initial density. A sampled observation of the common background is consistent with either field under noise of that size. After exact free evolution to t=0.2 their density difference has RMS 2.27 inside the halo interval. This is not an irrelevant global-phase ambiguity. Every deterministic predictor must miss at least one of the two targets by at least half their separation if both inputs are admissible.

This is a deliberately adversarial field, not a typical halo realization. It proves a conditional limitation, not a typical-error forecast. If the physical model excludes that field, or the state retains extra coefficients, moments, or history that distinguish it, the obstruction may disappear. Such extra information must be specified and tested. A grid selected by density alone also cannot distinguish these two initial states: their currents differ.

What a neural operator can and cannot change

A network need not invert a Fourier matrix. It can integrate local features, operate on a finite-element space, or use a learned coordinate map. Geo-FNO, JMLR 2023 learns such a deformation. GINO, NeurIPS 2023 combines weighted graph integration with a regular latent representation; its encoder uses geometric quadrature weights. NUNO, ICML 2023 partitions the domain and interpolates onto local regular grids. These are approaches to representing nonuniform information, not exceptions to the sampling bound.

On a nonuniform mesh, the discrete integral should contain physical volume weights: sum_j w_j k(x_i,x_j) f_j. A plain sample average instead integrates against the sampling distribution, overemphasizing the refined region. A volume L2 loss similarly needs weights; a deliberately halo-weighted loss is legitimate but defines a different objective. Correct weights are necessary for the intended integral, not sufficient for resolving fine phases. Fixed local neighborhoods also require care when the mesh changes.

Three guarantees must be kept separate: the architecture has a continuum limit; the trained continuum operator approximates the physical PDE; repeated application remains accurate over the required time. Resolution-independent parameters establish none of the latter two by themselves. The cross-resolution aliasing problem is explicitly studied in Discretization mismatch, ICLR 2025.

I used an analytically solvable learning control to isolate this issue from training difficulty. For Gaussian Fourier coefficients and Gaussian observation noise, the conditional mean is the optimal mean-square predictor among all estimators, including nonlinear neural networks. The noise standard deviation is 10⁻⁶ per volume-weighted sensor (not per raw point, and not the total vector norm). With training and test signals restricted to modes |k|≤8, the expected relative wave RMS is 4.13e-06. Apply the same optimal predictor to equal-power modes through |k|≤25 and it becomes 1.04. This is prior mismatch, not optimizer failure. An independent 4,000-draw Monte Carlo check validates one of the analytic risks to 0.13%.

Gravity is a different target from wave reconstruction. In Fourier space the mean-zero Poisson inverse attenuates a density mode by 1/k², whereas free Schrödinger evolution preserves its L2 amplitude. Consequently, some uncertainty in high-frequency density can be harmless for the potential. However, sparse samples can mix poorly observed combinations with low-frequency content: the selected near-null field has potential norm 0.106 despite sample norm 3.99e-08. One must test the composed density-to-potential map, not infer it from either smoothing or reconstruction conditioning alone. Here the Poisson control treats the field as a source perturbation; it is not a coupled nonlinear SP trajectory.

A relevant recent result is Patel, Subedi and Tewari, 2026 revision: a simple linear estimator exploits the structure of a fixed prescribed Schrödinger Hamiltonian and outperforms the compared FNO/DeepONet models on its examples. Its smoothness assumptions and restricted time-generalization results matter. Self-gravitating SP has a state-dependent Hamiltonian and nonlinear flow; the fixed-H result does not supply an SP solver. Similarly, preserving norm does not ensure the right phase: a learned energy error δω accumulates phase error approximately T δω even for a perfectly unitary propagator.

A missing symmetry test reverses the reference-H ranking

The original reference-H experiment strongly favored a stationary core, because the fixed reference potential was precisely the potential supporting that core. The perturbed example stayed near it. This was a useful mechanism test, but insufficient grounds to make it the general recommendation.

I now give that same self-consistent core quantized periodic velocities. Its continuum evolution is known independently: translate the stationary state and multiply by the Galilean phase. The 129-point and 257-point references agree to 8.81e-13 in density; a small-step fourth-order run agrees with the moving reference to 8.35e-12. A simpler candidate: one global high-order approximation space

A coherent alternative is a conforming high-order finite-element or spectral-element space on the refined mesh. Let M be its mass matrix, K its stiffness matrix, m_i=∫N_i, and b_i(c)=∫N_i|ψ_h|². Use the same space and physical quadrature for both fields:

i M dc/dt = [K/2 + V(Φ)] c
K Φ = -[b(c) - mean(ρ) m],       mᵀ Φ = 0
V_ij(Φ) = ∫ N_i Φ_h N_j dx

These equations use the dimensionless static normalization of the toys. Cosmological scale-factor coefficients would need their corresponding time dependence. The gravity source is integrated from the represented wave on active elements; it is not the squared amplitude of a lossy coarse complex restriction. Interelement coupling belongs to one assembled weak form. Multigrid levels, if used, are an algebraic solution hierarchy for this global problem and do not need independently evolved coarse wavefunctions as physical sources.

This is an established family: Auzinger et al., 2016 analyzes finite-element Strang splitting for SP under specific assumptions; Kormann, 2016 combines high-order elements with adaptive Schrödinger propagation; Li, Appelo and Zhang, 2021 studies spectral-element accuracy. MFEM performance documentation documents tensor-product matrix-free application and multigrid. These support feasibility of a 3D route, not a novelty claim or a performance prediction.

I implemented a small spatial control with one exact polynomial mass matrix and one stiffness matrix on ten graded periodic elements. It solves a manufactured Poisson field and propagates a free packet through changes in element size. Generalized eigendecomposition removes time error; independent fine quadrature measures physical error.

The element method conserves its discrete mass to roundoff, yet at degree eight its wave error is 3.5%. The packet moves and spreads into the coarse exterior. Raising degree resolves it, but the uniform Fourier baseline reaches much higher accuracy sooner. The scaled mass matrix stays well-conditioned (about 2), while the largest kinetic eigenvalue rises to 1.49×10⁵ at degree 24. Thus benign representation conditioning does not remove spatial approximation error or kinetic stiffness.

This prototype tests independent linear wave and gravity problems, not their nonlinear coupling. A complete Galerkin SP implementation must integrate the products accurately. With a consistent nondiagonal mass matrix, the projected potential subflow is generally not simply a pointwise phase kick; using such a kick without specifying collocation or mass lumping changes the method. This is precisely the kind of small-looking detail that can invalidate an apparently elegant combination.

Gaps that still matter

  1. We have no diagnosis of your halo discrepancy yet. The FD2 transfer error and controlled SP ablation make gravity a serious suspect. They do not prove it dominates GAMER's refinement, wave dispersion, boundary interpolation, filtering, subcycling, or cosmological errors. The inspected restriction guard has an exact cancellation defect in one branch, but its activation rate in your simulation is unknown. It does not establish loss of leaf-level mass. A source-level hypothesis must be instrumented before assigning physical blame.
  2. One-dimensional Poisson dynamics are not a radial three-dimensional soliton. The long-range kernel, phase-space population, mesh interfaces and communication costs change. None of these tests predicts the halo profile or a 3D speedup.
  3. Density bandwidth, wave bandwidth and granule size differ. Two waves generate difference-frequency density modes. The original padded source example demonstrates accurate source quadrature only; it does not prove that a coarse nonlinear phase kick evolves the projected SP system correctly. Dealiasing must include the wave-potential product, with measured memory and work. Points-per-wavelength figures need consistent definitions.
  4. The reference procedure must separate space, time and implementation. Earlier SP references used the same pseudospectral algorithm with simultaneous space/time refinement. Agreement is reassuring but cannot exclude shared bias. The new analytic moving-core reference and manufactured weak-form gravity test are independent controls. A production comparison still needs separate spatial, temporal and elliptic tolerance sweeps.
  5. Conservation and a small residual are necessary checks, not sufficient accuracy evidence. The band-limited and FD2 trajectories conserve their own discrete invariants while evolving the wrong solution. A local Poisson residual does not expose wrong boundary values; a global residual must also be interpreted in a physically relevant norm and alongside discretization error.
  6. A halo-selected mesh is a hypothesis about future wave complexity. Incoming currents, interference and low-density waves can matter later. A single CDM snapshot does not supply wave phase or guarantee adequate resolution along the full evolution. A prior CDM trajectory can help propose a mesh history, but it is not an accuracy certificate.
  7. Halo observables require their own convergence targets. Global density L2 can hide outer-halo errors; mass and fitted profile shape can hide wrong granule dynamics. Track core mass and radius, enclosed mass, velocity statistics, density spectra and profiles averaged with a consistent centering/time convention. Halo convergence target explicitly requires convergence throughout the halo and comparison with matched N-body results. A fit to some NFW profile is not sufficient, and agreement with CDM should not be imposed where the intended wave physics predicts a difference.
  8. Cost has not been demonstrated. Dense exponentials hide setup and action cost. Large nominal timesteps can move work into Krylov iterations, elliptic solves and global communication. Fixed-scale-factor toys omit time-dependent cosmological coefficients. A neural prediction benchmark must include data generation/training when relevant and test repeated rollout, unseen phases, boosts and changed refinement patterns.

The research decision after these controls

I would first test a single globally coupled high-order spatial discretization, with ordinary error-controlled time integration. Start from a fixed hierarchy that resolves a moving core and its wave bath, and solve gravity directly from that representation. Compare against uniform Fourier results at matched physical accuracy. Only then introduce mesh adaptation. This addresses the gravity/interface concern with one mathematical system rather than separate propagation rules patched together.

The next discriminating experiment is a self-gravitating core plus incoming interference crossing a grading interface, repeated under translations and boosts. Vary the mesh placement and polynomial order independently of time tolerance and Poisson tolerance. Measure global source moments, potential, phase-sensitive wave error and the core/halo observables. A method that wins only when the core sits on its favored location fails this gate. The finite-element toy here has not passed that full gate.

Keep the existing high-order FFT/splitting method as the baseline. Treat fixed-reference propagation as a conditional optimization, and neural operators as a separate hypothesis requiring explicit admissible-state and rollout tests. A learned initial guess or preconditioner can later be evaluated behind a converged global solve if it reduces measured work; learning is not needed for consistency. I would not make a coarse learned latent field the sole carrier of the resolved wave phase.

The unexplored contribution could be an accurate, efficient realization of the unified adaptive operator for cosmological SP. The components themselves have prior art. These results justify investigating that direction; they do not establish that it will beat GAMER, or that arbitrary timesteps become accurate.

  1. FINUFFT inverse tutorial. Exact sampling matrices can be ill-conditioned despite oversampling; gaps and the approximation space matter.

  8. Kormann, 2016. High-order Gauss-Lobatto finite elements and Magnus-Lanczos with adaptive error control for Schrodinger evolution.
  9. Li, Appelo and Zhang, 2021. Accuracy results for spectral elements applied to wave and linear Schrodinger equations.
