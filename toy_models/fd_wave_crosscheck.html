Does a resolved density profile hide the benefit of Gram-FE?

Yes, in the localized-core test with FD2 gravity. Once the wave error is below the common gravity error, the FD Laplacian-4 and Gram-FE profiles become very similar. Spectral gravity reveals the remaining wave-solver difference. Traveling interference remains a counterexample: its wave/density fields can differ substantially even when coarse averaged profiles appear close.

Exactly which algorithms were compared?

The cached GAMER CPU wave source, revision e36797538a1a5eaa456bc51ad44faf2b201a4aef, exposes LAPLACIAN_4TH off/on: the actual Laplacians are second-order (three points) and fourth-order (five points). We label these Laplacian-2 and Laplacian-4, avoiding an unsupported identification with a separate sixth-order Laplacian. This revision contains no sixth-order Laplacian branch in that solver. The Taylor update uses the default coefficient 1/6, with automatic optimization off as in the cached zoom example.

The FD half/full-step update and the optional face-flux amplitude correction are transcribed from the source. On the periodic grid, FFTs evaluate the finite-difference symbol exactly; this does not turn the derivative into a spectral derivative. An independent scalar real/imaginary implementation verifies the vectorized port. Both conservation settings are tested. No global normalization is applied.

Correction to the previous discussion: Gram-FE does not implement this local continuity correction. Its absence is intentional, not a missing part of our Gram-FE reconstruction. Unchanged FFT and MATMUL Gram-FE are the production-method controls here. A separately labeled experimental Gram-FE+FD4flux applies the same low-order flux/amplitude formula using a Gram-FE half-step predictor. It is not a GAMER option and is not proposed as a solver improvement.

All methods share initial samples, gravity, timestep and second-order Strang splitting at each grid. The comparison includes recorded accurate potential, self-consistent spectral gravity, and self-consistent FD2 gravity. Wave grids are uniform: no AMR wave transfer, refluxing, density floors, directional splitting, or cosmological expansion. The gravity Poisson solve is exact for its selected discrete symbol, excluding relaxation error.

Standing-wave limitation: the two dominant carriers have opposite k but equal kinetic frequency. A symmetric dispersion error shifts their common phase and largely cancels from their leading interference density. This makes that case useful for isolating gravity but a weak test of generic wave-solver equivalence. The moving/enveloped waves break this degeneracy.

Interpretation: the core result supports a shared gravity limitation as one explanation for similar high-resolution profiles. It does not establish that this is the dominant mechanism in GAMER halos. Spatial/time averaging, conservation corrections and refinement communication can also hide or replace the benefit of a more accurate wave solver.
