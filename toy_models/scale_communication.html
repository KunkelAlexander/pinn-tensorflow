How scales communicate

Third experimental round. Fixed nonuniform meshes, reversible coherent wave coordinates, full-density gravity, and independent controls for propagation and interfaces.

Finding: a single global, conservative Galerkin system still has substantial propagation error at modest order. The uniform-mesh controls show that this cannot all be blamed on the refinement boundary. Accurate gravity can be communicated without a coarse wavefunction, but this does not repair kinetic dispersion. The coupled solver converges on this benchmark; no 3D efficiency advantage has been demonstrated.

What was implemented

The equations are i psi_t = -psi_xx/2 + Phi psi and Phi_xx = |psi|² - mean(|psi|²), on a periodic interval of length 20. Elements have width 0.5 on [-5,0] and width 1 elsewhere. Translating the entire initial condition by -2, 0, or +2 leaves the continuum problem unchanged but changes its placement relative to the mesh. The 25-element graded mesh has 25p degrees of freedom at polynomial degree p.

A continuous polynomial space supplies an exactly integrated mass matrix M and stiffness matrix K. Generalized eigenvectors of K/2 against M give orthonormal coordinates a and kinetic eigenvalues E. All physical wave details represented in this space are retained. There are no separately evolved coarse waves, patch boundary predictions, mesh changes, or level subcycling.

psi at quadrature = Q a
b = Qᵀ W |Q a|²
Phi coefficients = G b,     G_j = -1/(2 E_j), G_0 = 0
i da/dt = E a + Qᵀ W [(Q G b) (Q a)]
energy = sum_j E_j |a_j|² + (1/2) bᵀ G b

The source and potential force use the same quadrature and energy functional. Gauss quadrature with 2p+4 points per element integrates the polynomial nonlinear loads exactly, up to roundoff. Linear propagation is exact in time for the discrete operator. Nonlinear propagation uses DOP853 in the kinetic interaction picture; it integrates the projected nonlinear equation, avoiding an unjustified pointwise phase kick for a consistent finite-element mass matrix. Dense matrices and eigensolves make this an accuracy oracle, not a scalable solver.

The equal-DOF uniform Fourier solver is also retained in the linear JSON. At 100 DOFs its error is approximately roundoff. That is an accuracy baseline for a small smooth 1D problem, not a proposed affordable replacement for a 3D zoom simulation. The present 2:1 mesh and limited localized complexity do not demonstrate the memory savings sought in a cosmological calculation.

3. A direct interface scattering control

To measure actual interface reflection separately, a monochromatic wave scatters between two semi-infinite uniform element chains. Condensing the interior of each element in (K/2 - E M) gives a two-face dynamic stiffness with diagonal a and off-diagonal b. The discrete propagation angle satisfies cos(theta)=-a/b, and the outgoing flux factor is Z=-b sin(theta). Then r=(Z_left-Z_right)/(Z_left+Z_right), R=|r|² and T=(Z_right/Z_left)|1+r|².

All probes use k h_coarse < pi on the first propagation band. This is an algebraic scattering calculation, with no packet projection or time error. The table fixes physical k=2.8 and a 2:1 refinement ratio. Ratios 4:1 and 8:1 are also included in the data.

The complete superposition, including interference, is normalized to mass 4. The problem is evolved to T=2 for all three spatial translations. What this changes

Keep the two communication problems separate: gravity can use accurate density loads or source moments; coherent propagation requires accurate reciprocal coupling and dispersion. A single global discretization removes certain inconsistent transfers but does not, by itself, deliver the accuracy gain sought in GAMER. The tests support the user's concern that Galerkin methods retain important difficulties.

Use the frequency response as an interface criterion: test reflected amplitude and transmitted phase at the same physical frequency on both sides. A conservative transfer can still couple two inaccurate propagation laws. The condensed interface response used above is a useful diagnostic, not a proposed production patch using a frequency-dependent solve for every mode.

No method winner yet: the coherent high-order system converges rapidly, but uniform Fourier is much more efficient in spatial DOFs on the linear toy. The experiments do not measure 3D work, communication, cosmological expansion, long-time relaxation, or halo density profiles. They do not reproduce GAMER's Gram-FE operator or diagnose its halo discrepancy. A 2:1 static mesh is far milder than a deep zoom hierarchy. Time-dependent mesh transfers and different level timesteps remain untested.

The next useful comparison would apply the same dispersion and interface-response measurements to the actual Gram-FE patch operator, while keeping a separate full-density Poisson reference. That would test whether its advertised local accuracy survives coupling between patches, without replacing the whole solver on the strength of a toy result.
